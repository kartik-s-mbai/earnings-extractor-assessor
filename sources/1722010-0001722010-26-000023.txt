Third Quarter 2026 vs. Second Quarter 2026
Net interest income increased by $2.1 million, or 11%, primarily driven by continued loan growth and the normalization of Federal Reserve Bank account income following a prior-quarter accrual adjustment. These benefits were partially offset by higher interest expense resulting from growth in average interest-bearing deposit balances. As a result, net interest margin increased by 19 basis points to 3.27%.
◦Interest-bearing Deposits in Other Banks: Interest income increased by $1.1 million, primarily reflecting the absence of a one-time Federal Reserve Bank accrual adjustment recorded in the prior quarter, as well as higher average balances.
◦Loans: Interest income increased by $1.9 million, driven largely by an $80.9 million or 4% increase in average loan balances, reflecting growth in CRE and home mortgage loans, along with a 5-basis-point increase in average loan yield to 6.41%.
◦Deposits: Interest expense increased by $1.0 million, primarily due to a $76.4 million or 4% increase in average interest-bearing deposit balances, including growth in money market and time deposit accounts. Despite the higher balances, the average cost of interest-bearing deposits increased only 2 basis points to 3.79%, reflecting relatively stable funding costs during the quarter.
Third Quarter 2026 vs. Third Quarter 2025
Net interest income increased by $1.9 million, or 9%, primarily driven by balance sheet growth and lower deposit rates. These favorable factors were partially offset by lower loan yields. As a result, the net interest margin increased by one basis point to 3.27%.
◦Loans: Interest income increased by $2.6 million, largely attributable to a $201.9 million increase in average loan balances, reflecting continued growth in CRE loans. The increase was partially offset by an 11-basis-point decline in loan yields, reflecting the impact of the federal funds rate cuts during the prior year and the repricing of adjustable-rate loans and newly originated loans.
◦Deposits: Interest expense increased by $463 thousand, mainly due to a $173.3 million increase in average interest-bearing deposit balances, driven mainly by growth in time deposits. The increase was partially offset by a 28-basis-point decline in costs of interest-bearing deposits, reflecting the repricing of time deposits and money market accounts following the federal funds rate cuts.

Overall credit quality remained generally sound during the quarter. We believe the allowance for credit losses on loans remained adequate at 1.26% of gross loans as of September 30, 2026.
◦Accruing loans 30-89 days past-due increased by $1.7 million, primarily driven by two CRE loans totaling $7.7 million moving into this category, partially offset by $6.3 million of loans returning to less than 30 days past due, primarily home mortgage and SBA loans.
◦Nonperforming loans increased by $768 thousand, primarily driven by $2.2 million of loans migrating into nonaccrual status, partially offset by a $1.1 million decrease in the balances of existing nonperforming loans.
◦Criticized loans increased by $44.1 million, primarily driven by $45.7 million of loans newly entering the category during the quarter. The new additions were primarily CRE loans rated Special Mention.
◦Special Mention loans increased by $42.9 million, primarily driven by four relationships totaling $41.5 million. An $8.2 million C&I relationship and a $5.9 million CRE relationship were downgraded primarily due to DCR covenant shortfalls but remain current. A $6.2 million CRE loan is in early delinquency due to sponsor liquidity constraints, while a $21.3 million CRE developer relationship in downtown Los Angeles remains current and performing, with the property being marketed for sale. Management continues to closely monitor these relationships and believes there is a viable path toward a positive resolution through repayment or credit upgrades over the next few quarters.

ABOUT OP BANCORP
