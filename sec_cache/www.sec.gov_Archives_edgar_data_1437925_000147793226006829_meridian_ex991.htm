First, non-cash impairment charges. We recorded $11.5 million in non-cash impairment charges during the quarter, which significantly impacted our reported. Of this amount, $5.5 million related to the full write-off of our Brazilian gaming license and associated intangible assets following Brazil’s ban on fixed-odds betting, including online casino. We suspended our operations in Brazil in line with the ban, and we don’t expect to resume them during the rest of 2026. We intend to stay in Brazil while Congress and the courts decide on the ban, with a limited local presence, and office and minimal operating costs. Another $5.9 million related to goodwill impairment in the reporting unit comprising GMAG, our aggregation business, RKings, and Classics for a Cause, reflecting continued operating losses. Importantly, these charges were entirely non-cash and did not affect our operating cash flow during the quarter.

 

Second, customer-favorable sports results. Online sports betting revenue fell 15%, or $1.6 million, to $8.7 million, as results that favored our customers lowered our online sports betting margin to 7.4%, compared with 8.2% a year ago. Compared with the second quarter, online sports betting revenue declined by $4.4 million, while betting volume was broadly stable.

 

Third, higher operating costs. Our selling, general and administrative expenses increased 5% year-over-year, while revenue declined 3%. The increase was mainly driven by $0.9 million in higher bad-debt expense, primarily in GMAG's B2B gaming content resale business, $0.4 million in additional marketing around FIFA World Cup, and $0.4 million in higher rent and utilities from the new betting shops, and $0.3 million in higher salaries, along with approximately $1 million in other operating costs, partly offset by approximately $1.6 million in lower depreciation, amortization, professional fees, and stock-based compensations.

 

Despite these challenges, our core business remains healthy. In sports betting, online revenue rose 12% to $33.5 million over the first nine months, helped by wider live streaming coverage. Our online casino business continued to perform well. Online casino revenue grew to $17.1 million, compared with $14.5 million in the second quarter and $14.6 million a year ago. Our cash generation remains strong, and our balance sheet continues to strengthen. Over the first nine months, we generated $17.7 million in operating cash flow, up 3% year-over-year. This funded all our investments and supported $9 million in debt repayments, without raising additional equity capital. Also, we reduced net debt for the seventh consecutive quarter.

 

Now, onto the individual business segments.

 

Meridianbet Group is 72% of our business. Its revenue rose 2% to $33.1 million, but operating income fell to $0.7 million from $4.6 million, because of the sports betting margin and higher costs. Online casino led the growth, with revenue up 17% to $17.1 million, and up 21% over nine months, as we added new game providers in the quarter, and now offer more than 2,500 games. Customer deposits rose 9% to $101.1 million, and the average deposit per depositor rose 18%.

 

In retail, casino revenue rose 24% over nine months, helped by more than 100 new slot machines, and in the third quarter we opened two new betting shops in Serbia and moved a third to a larger location. New and larger shops take time to show their full contribution, and we judge each one of them on its own demand, costs, and returns. New registrations rose 25% in the third quarter, to 458,680.

 

Expanse Studios, our game studio, grew revenue from other operators 114%, with players up 52%, and it released 14 new games in the quarter. At September 30, it had 101 proprietary titles, and its games were live on more than 2,000 operator sites, against 1,881 in the second quarter. In the third quarter, it received further certification in Sweden, and our applications in New Jersey and Ontario are awaiting regulatory approval. We have done everything required on our side.

 

At RKings and Classics for a Cause, our prize competition business in the U.K. and our trade promotion business in Australia, combined revenue fell 19% to $9.1 million. Even so, the segment's operating result improved from a small loss in the second quarter to a small profit in the third quarter. We will build on that with strong offer for customers and tight control of acquisition and promotional costs. In Australia, the law that governs trade promotions changes on January 1, 2027. We are working to modify the way Classics for a Cause does business there so that it complies, and that work is in progress.

For the first nine months, total revenue increased 10% to $146.3 million, with Africa growing 33% and Central and South America growing 27%.

 

Moving to gross profit, we reported $24.8 million for the quarter, compared with $26.4 million a year ago. Gross margin declined to 54% from 55.8%, primarily reflecting lower sports betting margins. For the first nine months, gross profit increased 6% to $79.8 million.

 

Turning to operating expenses, SG&A increased 5% to $28.2 million, compared with $26.8 million a year ago. This increase included $0.9 million in higher bad debt expense, $0.4 million in additional marketing, mainly around the World Cup, $0.4 million in higher rent and utilities from new betting shops, and $0.3 million in higher salaries. Other operating costs increased approximately $1 million. These increases were partially offset by approximately $1.6 million in lower depreciation, amortization, professional, and stock-based compensation expenses.

 

Looking at the first nine months, we maintained good cost discipline. Marketing expenses remained flat, professional fees declined 6%, and stock-based compensation decreased approximately $2.5 million, mainly due to fewer awards granted and the reversal of previously accrued executive share compensation in June.

 

Overall, nine-month SG&A expenses were slightly lower at $77.6 million, compared to $77.8 million last year, despite revenue growing 10%.

 

Moving to impairment charges, we recorded $11.5 million during the quarter, all non-cash. In Brazil, we wrote off $4.1 million related to our gaming license and $1.4 million related to the associated domain and player database. We also recorded a $5.9 million goodwill impairment in the reporting unit comprising GMAG, RKings, and Classics for a Cause, reflecting continued operating losses. Following this impairment, $2 million of goodwill remains in this reporting unit. These charges had no impact on our cash flow during the quarter. Interest expense declined $0.2 million from $0.5 million a year ago and was down 74% to $0.9 million for the first nine months.
