Financial Results for the Third Quarter of 2026

Net sales for the third quarter of 2026 increased $20.5 million, or 4.7%, to $459.8 million from $439.3 million for the third quarter of 2025. The increase was primarily attributable to an increase in base business net sales, three months of net sales for the College Inn and Kitchen Basics brands and three months of net sales from the co-manufacturing agreement the Company entered into with the acquirer of the Green Giant U.S. frozen business, partially offset by the Green Giant U.S. frozen and Le Sueur U.S. divestitures.

​

​

Base business net sales for the third quarter of 2026 increased $19.4 million, or 5.0%, to $404.6 million from $385.2 million for the third quarter of 2025. The increase in base business net sales was driven by an increase in volume of $23.6 million, or 6.1% of base business net sales, partially offset by a decrease in net pricing and the impact of product mix of $3.3 million, or 0.9% of base business net sales, and the negative impact of foreign currency of $0.9 million, or 0.2% of base business net sales.

Net sales for the recently acquired College Inn and Kitchen Basics brands contributed $29.0 million to the Company’s net sales for the third quarter of 2026 and net sales of the Company’s Green Giant U.S. frozen co-manufacturing agreement contributed $26.3 million of net sales in the third quarter of 2026. These net sales were partially offset by the Green Giant U.S. frozen business and the Le Sueur U.S. business, which contributed net sales during the third quarter of 2025 of $51.8 million and $2.3 million, respectively, which the Company divested in the first quarter of 2026 and in fiscal 2025, respectively, and are therefore not part of the Company’s third quarter of 2026 results.

For the third quarter of 2026, gross profit was $108.9 million, or 23.7% of net sales, and adjusted gross profit(1) was $111.2 million, or 24.2% of net sales. For the third quarter of 2025, gross profit was $99.0 million, or 22.5% of net sales, and adjusted gross profit was $98.8 million, or 22.5% of net sales. Gross profit as a percentage of net sales and adjusted gross profit percentage increased primarily due to the acquisition of the higher margin College Inn and Kitchen Basics brands and the divestiture of the lower margin Green Giant U.S. frozen business. Gross profit and adjusted gross profit also increased in part due to increased base business net sales and lower tariff expense, including the effect of certain tariff refunds received from the U.S. government.

Selling, general and administrative expenses increased $6.7 million, or 14.9%, to $51.3 million for the third quarter of 2026 from $44.6 million for the third quarter of 2025. The increase was composed of increases in acquisition/divestiture-related and non-recurring expenses of $7.9 million, consumer marketing expenses of $0.6 million and warehousing expenses of $0.3 million, partially offset by decreases in selling expenses of $1.1 million and general and administrative expenses of $1.0 million. Expressed as a percentage of net sales, selling, general and administrative expenses increased by 1.0 percentage point to 11.2% for the third quarter of 2026, as compared to 10.2% for the third quarter of 2025.

During the third quarter of 2026, the Company recorded pre-tax, non-cash impairment charges of $40.7 million related to indefinite-lived intangible trademark assets for the B&M and Joan of Arc brands in connection with the Company’s divestiture of those brands during the fourth quarter of 2026.

During the third quarter of 2025, the Company recorded pre-tax, non-cash impairment charges of $26.0 million related to indefinite-lived intangible trademark assets for the Victoria and McCann’s brands.

During the third quarter of 2026, the Company recorded a gain of approximately $1.8 million related to the Green Giant Canada(2) assets held for sale. The gain resulted from a change in the estimated fair value of the Green Giant Canada assets held for sale less the estimated cost to sell those assets. Assets held for sale are remeasured quarterly.

During the third quarter of 2025, the Company completed the Le Sueur U.S. divestiture and recognized a gain on sale of $15.5 million. During the third quarter of 2025, the Company recorded pre-tax, non-cash impairment charges of $27.8 million related to the Green Giant Canada assets held for sale.

Net interest expense increased $7.5 million, or 20.1%, to $44.8 million for the third quarter of 2026 from $37.3 million for the third quarter of 2025. The increase was primarily attributable to an increase in average long-term debt outstanding compared to the third quarter of 2025, the 11.00% interest rate on the Company’s new senior notes due 2031, and a loss on extinguishment of debt of $1.1 million during the third quarter of 2026 compared to a net gain on extinguishment of debt of $0.5 million during the third quarter of 2025.

The Company had a net loss of $32.5 million, or $0.40 per diluted share, for the third quarter of 2026, compared to a net loss of $19.1 million, or $0.24 per diluted share, for the third quarter of 2025.

The Company’s adjusted net income for the third quarter of 2026 was $17.8 million, or $0.21 per adjusted diluted share, compared to adjusted net income of $11.7 million, or $0.15 per adjusted diluted share, for the third quarter of 2025.

Adjusted EBITDA was $81.8 million for the third quarter of 2026 compared to $70.4 million for the third quarter of 2025. Adjusted EBITDA as a percentage of net sales was 17.8% for the third quarter of 2026, compared to 16.0% for the third quarter of 2025.

The increases in adjusted diluted earnings per share, adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA as a percentage of net sales were primarily attributable to increased base business net sales, the acquisition of the College Inn and Kitchen Basics brands, the divestiture of the Green Giant U.S. frozen business, the commencement of the Green Giant U.S. frozen co-manufacturing business, and lower tariff expenses, including the effect of certain tariff refunds received from the U.S. government.

Financial Results for the First Three Quarters of 2026

Net sales for the first three quarters of 2026 decreased $37.1 million, or 2.9%, to $1,252.0 million from $1,289.1 million for the first three quarters of 2025. The decrease was primarily attributable to the Green Giant U.S. frozen, Le Sueur U.S. and Don Pepino divestitures, partially offset by seven months of net sales from the co-manufacturing agreement the Company entered into on March 2, 2026 with the acquirer of the Green Giant U.S. frozen business, six-and-a-half months of net sales for the College Inn and Kitchen Basics brands, and an increase in base business net sales.

As a result of the divestiture of the Green Giant U.S. frozen business on March 2, 2026, net sales from the Green Giant U.S. frozen business were $137.5 million lower during the first three quarters of 2026 than during the first three quarters of 2025. Net sales of the Don Pepino and Le Sueur U.S. businesses, which the Company divested in 2025 and are therefore not part of the Company’s first three quarters of 2026 results, were $22.6 million during the first three quarters of 2025. Partially offsetting the impact of these divestitures were seven months of net sales from the Green Giant U.S. frozen co-manufacturing agreement, which contributed $58.7 million of net sales in the first three quarters of 2026, and six-and-a-half months of net sales for the College Inn and Kitchen Basics brands, acquired on March 19, 2026, which contributed $45.1 million to the Company’s net sales for the first three quarters of 2026.

Base business net sales for the first three quarters of 2026 increased $19.7 million, or 1.8%, to $1,116.0 million from $1,096.3 million for the first three quarters of 2025. The increase in base business net sales was driven by an increase in volume of $15.2 million, or 1.4% of base business net sales, an increase in net pricing and the impact of product mix (primarily related to the Spices & Flavor Solutions business unit) of $3.4 million, or 0.3% of base business net sales, and the positive impact of foreign currency of $1.1 million, or 0.1% of base business net sales.

For the first three quarters of 2026, gross profit was $268.5 million or 21.4% of net sales, and adjusted gross profit was $279.4 million, or 22.3% of net sales. For the first three quarters of 2025, gross profit was $276.1 million, or 21.4% of net sales, and adjusted gross profit was $278.5 million, or 21.6% of net sales. Adjusted gross profit percentage increased due to the acquisition of the higher margin College Inn and Kitchen Basics brands, the divestiture of the lower margin Green Giant U.S. frozen business, and lower tariff expenses during the first three quarters of 2026 compared to the first three quarters of 2025, including the effect of certain tariff refunds received from the U.S. government.

Selling, general and administrative expenses increased $1.2 million, or 0.8%, to $142.1 million for the first three quarters of 2026 from $140.9 million for the first three quarters of 2025. The increase was composed of an increase in acquisition/divestiture-related and non-recurring expenses of $16.6 million, inclusive of an increase of $1.9 million for disposals and impairments of property, plant and equipment, partially offset by decreases in general and administrative expenses of $7.5 million, warehousing expenses of $4.9 million, selling expenses of $1.8 million and consumer marketing expenses of $1.2 million. Expressed as a percentage of net sales, selling, general and administrative expenses increased by 0.4 percentage points to 11.3% for the first three quarters of 2026, as compared to 10.9% for the first three quarters of 2025.

During the first three quarters of 2026, the Company recorded pre-tax, non-cash impairment charges of $40.7 million related to indefinite-lived intangible trademark assets for the B&M and Joan of Arc brands in connection with the Company’s divestiture of those brands during the fourth quarter of 2026.

During the first three quarters of 2025, the Company recorded pre-tax, non-cash impairment charges of $26.0 million related to indefinite-lived intangible trademark assets for the Victoria and McCann’s brands.

During the first three quarters of 2026, the Company recognized a loss on sale of assets of $36.3 million, primarily related to the divestiture of the Green Giant U.S. frozen business.

During the first three quarters of 2026, the Company recorded a gain of approximately $1.8 million related to the Green Giant Canada assets held for sale. Net interest expense increased $8.3 million, or 7.4%, to $119.1 million for the first three quarters of 2026 from $110.8 million for the first three quarters of 2025. The increase was primarily attributable to an increase in average long-term debt outstanding during the first three quarters of 2026 compared to the first three quarters of 2025, the 11.00% interest rate on the Company’s new senior notes due 2031, and a loss on extinguishment of debt of $1.1 million during the first three quarters of 2026 compared to a net gain on extinguishment of debt of $2.3 million during the first three quarters of 2025. During the first three quarters of 2026, net interest expense was also negatively impacted in connection with the Company’s debt refinancing because the Company’s 11.00% senior notes due 2031 were issued on June 10, 2026, prior to the redemption of the Company’s 5.25% senior notes due 2027, and therefore during a 24-day period, the Company incurred interest expense on both sets of notes, which was only partially offset by interest earned on the net proceeds of the issuance of the 11.00% senior notes due 2031.

The Company had a net loss of $69.0 million, or $0.85 per diluted share, for the first three quarters of 2026, compared to a net loss of $28.1 million, or $0.35 per diluted share, for the first three quarters of 2025. The Company’s larger net loss for the first three quarters of 2026 as compared to the first three quarters of 2025 was primarily attributable to: the pre-tax, non-cash impairment charges of $40.7 million related to indefinite-lived intangible trademark assets for the B&M and Joan of Arc brands in connection with the Company’s divestiture of those brands during the fourth quarter of 2026, the loss on sale of assets of $36.3 million (primarily related to the divestiture of the Green Giant U.S. frozen business during the first quarter of 2026), and an increase in acquisition/divestiture-related and non-recurring expenses, partially offset by the gain of approximately $1.8 million related to the Green Giant Canada assets held for sale recorded during the third quarter of 2026 as compared to the pre-tax, non-cash impairment charges of $27.8 million related to the Green Giant Canada assets held for sale during the third quarter of 2025.

The Company’s adjusted net income for the first three quarters of 2026 was $29.5 million, or $0.36 per adjusted diluted share, compared to adjusted net income of $18.1 million, or $0.23 per adjusted diluted share, for the first three quarters of 2025. The increase in adjusted net income and adjusted diluted earnings per share in the first three quarters of 2026 was primarily attributable to the factors described above, and a decrease in depreciation and amortization.

For the first three quarters of 2026, adjusted EBITDA was $199.9 million, an increase of $12.4 million, or 6.6%, compared to $187.5 million for the first three quarters of 2025. Adjusted EBITDA as a percentage of net sales was 16.0% for the first three quarters of 2026, compared to 14.5% for the first three quarters of 2025.

(1)EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA are non-GAAP financial measures used by management to measure operating performance. A non-GAAP financial measure is defined as a numerical measure of the Company’s financial performance that excludes or includes amounts so as to be different from the most directly comparable measure calculated and presented in accordance with GAAP in the Company’s consolidated balance sheets and related consolidated statements of operations, comprehensive loss, changes in stockholders’ equity and cash flows. The Company defines EBITDA as net income (loss) before net interest expense, income taxes, and depreciation and amortization. The Company defines adjusted EBITDA as EBITDA adjusted for cash and non-cash acquisition/divestiture-related expenses, gains and losses (which may include third-party fees and expenses, integration, restructuring and consolidation expenses, amortization of acquired inventory fair value step-up, and gains and losses on the sale of certain assets); gains and losses on extinguishment of debt; impairment of assets held for sale; impairment of intangible assets; and non-recurring expenses, gains and losses.

Management believes that it is useful to eliminate these items because it allows management to focus on what it deems to be a more reliable indicator of ongoing operating performance and the Company’s ability to generate cash flow from operations. The Company uses EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA in the Company’s business operations to, among other things, evaluate the Company’s operating performance, develop budgets and measure the Company’s performance against those budgets, determine employee bonuses and evaluate the Company’s cash flows in terms of cash needs. The Company also presents EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA because the Company believes they are useful indicators of the Company’s historical debt capacity and ability to service debt and because covenants in the Company’s credit agreement, the Company’s senior secured notes indenture and the Company’s senior notes indenture contain ratios based on these measures. As a result, reports used by internal management during monthly operating reviews feature the EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA metrics. However, management uses these metrics in conjunction with traditional GAAP operating performance and liquidity measures as part of its overall assessment of the Company’s operating performance, cash needs and ability to service debt, and therefore does not place undue reliance on these measures.

EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA are not recognized terms under GAAP and do not purport to be alternatives to operating income (loss), net income (loss) or any other GAAP measure as indicators of operating performance. EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA are not measures of cash flows in accordance with GAAP and should not be considered as alternatives to net cash provided by operating activities or any other measure of liquidity determined in accordance with GAAP. EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA do not reflect the Company’s cash requirements for debt service, working capital, capital expenditures, acquisitions, income taxes or dividends. Rather, management believes EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA may provide useful information regarding the Company’s ability to fund these cash requirements. EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA are not complete measures of the Company’s profitability because they do not include certain costs and expenses and gains and losses described above. Because not all companies use identical calculations, the Company’s presentation of EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA may not be comparable to similarly titled measures used by other companies. However, management believes EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA can be useful in evaluating the Company’s performance relative to peer companies because these measures provide users with additional insight into key components of the Company’s results determined in accordance with GAAP.

(1)Adjusted gross profit and adjusted gross profit percentage are non-GAAP financial measures used by management to measure operating performance. The Company defines adjusted gross profit as gross profit adjusted for acquisition/divestiture-related expenses and non-recurring expenses included in cost of goods sold, and adjusted gross profit percentage as gross profit as a percentage of net sales adjusted for acquisition/divestiture-related expenses and non-recurring expenses included in cost of goods sold. These non-GAAP financial measures reflect adjustments to gross profit and gross profit as a percentage of net sales to eliminate the items identified in the reconciliation above. This information is provided in order to allow investors to make meaningful comparisons of the Company’s operating performance between periods and to view the Company’s business from the same perspective as the Company’s management. Because the Company cannot predict the timing and amount of these items, management does not consider these items when evaluating the Company’s performance or when making decisions regarding allocation of resources.
